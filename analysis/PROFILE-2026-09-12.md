## vLLM flashinfer (non-speculative) regression — root cause

GPU span only 4,089 µs/step, yet the server decodes at ~10 ms/token: **~6 ms/step is host-side**.
Per-step CPU work (relative shares from the trace): torch.compile piecewise-graph dispatch ~40%,
`moe_forward_shared` ~22%, `qwen_gdn_attention_core` (triton GDN launches) ~19%, `bmm_fp8` ~11%.
GPU-side, FP8 projections dispatch to **legacy sm89 cuBLAS kernels** (~1.25 ms/step) at M=1 instead
of the nvjet sm120 path the MTP5 verify pass gets, and vLLM's shared-expert Marlin runs at
~0.12 TB/s. Conclusion: this config is currently both host-bound and kernel-regressed. The recorded
244 tok/s already regressed vs 0.26's 299. **Do not build on vLLM's non-speculative path; SGLang's
is strictly healthier (329 tok/s, 99% GPU busy).** Whether today's extra host overhead vs the
recorded run is environmental (CPU state) was not isolated — it does not change the verdict.

## Roofline (batch one, derived from config.json; `analysis/roofline.py`)

Per-token HBM traffic: weights 2.06 GiB (FP8 attn projections 1.14, routed experts 0.53, lm_head
0.27, shared expert 0.07, router 0.04) + recurrent state r/w 0.12 GiB (fp32) + KV 2.5/40/160 MiB at
128/2K/8K. Ceilings at 1.79 TB/s theoretical peak: **782 / 769 / 729 tok/s**. SGLang's 329 tok/s at
128 in = 2.29 GB/3.04 ms ≈ **0.75 TB/s = 42% of peak**.

Conditional budgets for SGLang's step (if each group reaches ~1.5 TB/s, ~85% peak):

| Change | Save µs/token | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| Marlin MoE 714 → 355 | ~360 | 1.14× |
| + Marlin shared/lm_head 694 → 225 | ~460 | 1.34× |
| + FP8 922 → 750, recurrent 185 → 81 | ~230 | 1.46× |
| + fuse half of elementwise/memcpy/routing/quant/reduction (910 µs) | ~450 | **1.75× (~575 tok/s)** |

All-Marlin NVFP4 work (1.41 ms/step, 47% of span) at 27–42% of peak is the standout target —
Marlin's M=1 GEMV leaves 2–3.5× of bandwidth on the table. The conditional 2× ceiling needs
essentially everything at ~90% of peak plus deep fusion; 1.5–1.75× is the plausible engineered range
for a specialized batch-one decode path. These are conditional budget calculations, not predictions.

## Ranked kernel targets (supersedes the old vLLM-trace list for the SGLang baseline)
1. **Batch-one NVFP4 GEMV/dequant for experts, shared expert, lm_head** (1.41 ms/step at 27–42% of
   peak). Router→expert→reduction fusion rides on top (+0.17 ms routing+reduction layout overhead).
   Success metric: ≥1.3 TB/s on 540 MiB expert traffic → ~360 µs saved per token.
2. **Fuse the small-op tail** (norms/act/quant/copy/routing: ~0.85 ms/step, 864+ sub-5µs kernels)
   into the GEMMs around them. Quant (112 µs) + gemvx b/a projections (181 µs at 7% of peak) are
   cheap wins inside the linear-attention block.
3. **Recurrent state traffic** (185 µs at 37% of peak): fp32 state r+w is 120 MiB/token; fp16/bf16
   state or fused read-modify-write could halve it — check numerics first (mamba_ssm_dtype=fp32 is
   deliberate).
4. **MTP-specific**: verify-pass attention cost at ≥2K context (why MTP loses at 8K); draft bf16
   tax (~0.55 ms/token accepted). An adaptive context-threshold policy remains the cheap fix.
